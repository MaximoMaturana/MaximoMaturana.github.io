CareBot

Conceptual design of an autonomous follow-me medical supply carrier for hospital ward rounds — applying the VDI 2221 systematic design methodology end-to-end.

Overview

What does it do?

CareBot is a conceptual autonomous follow-me robot designed to assist hospital staff during ward rounds. Nurses and doctors spend a significant portion of their time transporting supplies between departments — CareBot addresses this by following a healthcare worker, carrying up to 30 kg of medical supplies, and stopping safely the moment any obstacle is detected.

The project applies the VDI 2221 systematic design methodology from problem definition through detailed engineering analysis. Three drivetrain concepts were evaluated via a weighted decision matrix — a differential drive platform was selected for its simplicity, reliability, and suitability for indoor hospital environments. Estimated prototype cost: ~€865.

Key Specs

  • ⚖️ Max payload: 30 kg
  • 🏃 Max speed: 0.8 m/s
  • 🔋 Battery: 24 V, 20 Ah (~4 h)
  • 📏 Robot width: ≤ 600 mm
  • 🧠 Controller: ESP32
  • ⚡ Motor driver: Cytron MDDS30
  • 👁️ Sensors: 6× VL53L1X ToF
  • 💶 Prototype cost: ~€865

Design Process — VDI 2221

How it all fits together

01

Problem Definition

Identified that hospital staff spend significant time on manual logistics. User needs defined: minimal interaction, safe navigation, reliable following, organized storage, easy-to-clean surfaces.

→
02

Functional Structure

System inputs (power, user signal, obstacle data) and outputs (motion, supply transport, safe stop) mapped. Decomposed into sub-functions: store, power, detect, process, move.

→
03

Concept Evaluation

Three drivetrain architectures compared via weighted decision matrix across cost, reliability, complexity, efficiency, and manufacturability. Differential drive scored highest (21/25).

→
04

Engineering Calculations

Wheel speed (102 RPM), motor torque (2.37 Nm), battery runtime (4 h at 120 W avg), and encoder resolution (≥100 PPR) all derived analytically to validate component selection.

→
05

Safety & Feasibility

Multi-layer safety: 6× ToF sensors, front bumper switches, emergency stop, current limiting, auto-stop on signal loss. Technical, economic, and manufacturing feasibility confirmed.

System Highlights

Key design decisions

🤖

Differential Drive

Two 24 V, 90 W gearmotors with 5.63 Nm torque controlled via Cytron MDDS30. Front caster wheels ensure smooth, stable manoeuvring in narrow hospital corridors.

🔭

Multi-Layer Safety

Six VL53L1X time-of-flight sensors provide 2 m obstacle detection range. Front bumper microswitches act as a final hardware safety layer triggering an immediate stop.

🧠

ESP32 Controller

Single ESP32 manages sensor data, motor PWM signals, encoder feedback, and Bluetooth follow-me communication — all simultaneously at prototype level.

🏥

Hospital-Ready Design

HDPE cabinet panels — durable, chemically resistant, and easy to clean. Aluminium structural frame. Multiple drawers with telescopic ball-bearing slides for organized supply access.
